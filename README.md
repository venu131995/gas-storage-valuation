# Natural Gas Storage Valuation — Jump-Diffusion Model + Least-Squares Monte Carlo

A gas storage facility is a real option on the shape and volatility of the price curve: buy and
inject when gas is cheap, withdraw and sell when it is expensive, subject to physical limits on how
fast gas can move in and out. This project values such a facility on **Henry Hub** spot prices and
tests the resulting trading policy **out of sample** on a real gas year.

**Pipeline**

1. **Price model.** Seasonal mean-reverting model for log prices, calibrated by exact maximum likelihood
   on daily Henry Hub data (FRED `DHHNGSP`, 2010 onward). Two specifications are fitted and compared:
   - one-factor Schwartz / Ornstein–Uhlenbeck with Fourier seasonality;
   - **mean-reverting jump-diffusion (MRJD)**, which adds compound-Poisson price spikes.
2. **Intrinsic value.** Dynamic programming over a 61-point inventory grid against the model's expected
   price curve — the value of the best fixed schedule.
3. **Total value (intrinsic + extrinsic).** **Least-Squares Monte Carlo** (Longstaff–Schwartz, in the
   storage form of Boogert & de Jong, 2008): backward induction on 20,000 simulated paths, with the value
   of each inventory level estimated by regressing realised future cash flows on polynomials of the price
   state. An independent set of paths is then run through the fitted rule to give a low-biased estimate.
4. **Out-of-sample backtest.** Calibrate only on data up to 28 Mar 2024, then trade the April 2024 –
   March 2025 gas year on *realised* prices with the frozen policy. Compare with the intrinsic schedule and
   with the perfect-foresight upper bound.
5. **Risk and sensitivity.** Value against volatility and against injection/withdrawal speed, plus the
   optimal decision rule (inject / hold / withdraw) as a function of price and inventory.

## Facility

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Working gas capacity | 1,000,000 MMBtu (1 Bcf) |
| Max injection / withdrawal | full in 60 trading days / empty in 30 trading days |
| Variable costs | USD 0.02/MMBtu injection, USD 0.01/MMBtu withdrawal, 1% fuel loss on injection |
| Contract | starts empty, must finish empty; 4% discount rate; daily decisions |

## Results

All figures come from `results/summary.json`, generated by `scripts/run_valuation.py`.

### 1. The jump model fits Henry Hub far better

| Model (calibrated 2010 – Sep 2026) | κ (1/yr) | Half-life | σ diffusion | Jumps/yr | Jump s.d. | AIC |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gaussian OU | 6.66 | 38 days | 1.25 | – | – | −10,107 |
| **MRJD** | **2.13** | **119 days** | **0.59** | **18.5** | **0.24** | **−13,964** |

The Gaussian model has to inflate its volatility to 125% a year to explain weather-driven spikes such as
Winter Storm Uri (Feb 2021). Separating the spikes into jumps cuts diffusive volatility to 59% and improves
AIC by about **3,860 points**. Most of the extra fit is in the tails (`fig0_jump_vs_gaussian.png`).

### 2. Out-of-sample backtest: gas year April 2024 – March 2025

Calibrated on 2010 – Mar 2024 only, then traded on realised prices (spot ranged USD 1.21 – 9.86):

| Strategy (realised, discounted) | USD m |
|---|---|
| Perfect foresight (theoretical upper bound) | 3.36 |
| **LSMC policy — MRJD** | **1.60** |
| LSMC policy — Gaussian OU | 1.79 |
| Intrinsic (fixed) schedule | 1.11 |

The dynamic MRJD policy earned **44% more than the fixed schedule** and captured **48% of the
perfect-foresight maximum**. It kept gas in storage through the January 2025 cold snap and withdrew into
the price spikes, instead of emptying on a fixed calendar (`fig4_backtest.png`).

The Gaussian-model policy did slightly better in this year. That is one realised path, so it is not
evidence against the better-fitting model. A fair comparison would repeat the backtest over many years
(see *Extensions*).

### 3. Current valuation: 12 months from 30 Sep 2026 (MRJD)

| | USD m | ± s.e. |
|---|---|---|
| Intrinsic value | 0.41 | – |
| LSMC, in-sample | 1.10 | 0.012 |
| **LSMC, out-of-sample (lower bound)** | **1.11** | 0.012 |
| Extrinsic share of value | 63% | |

In- and out-of-sample estimates agree within one standard error, so the regression-based policy is not
overfitting. Most of the facility's value is **optionality**, not the seasonal spread. With spot at USD 3.18
close to its long-run level and only a ~17% seasonal peak-to-trough, the locked-in spread is small.

## Method notes

- **Exact discretisation.** The OU transition over any gap Δt is Gaussian with variance
  σ²(1 − e^{−2κΔt})/(2κ), so weekends and holidays are handled exactly in both likelihood and simulation.
- **Jump likelihood.** At most one jump per observation interval, giving a two-component Gaussian-mixture
  density maximised with Nelder–Mead followed by BFGS. Seasonality is estimated first by OLS on Fourier terms.
- **Forward curve with jumps.** E[S(t)] combines the lognormal diffusion moment with
  exp(λ∫₀^τ (M_J(e^{−κu}) − 1) du) for decaying compound-Poisson jumps. It is verified against Monte Carlo in
  the tests.
- **LSMC.** Cubic polynomial basis in the standardised state; one regression per date and *target* inventory
  level; realised rather than fitted cash flows are propagated backwards; antithetic variates.
- **Tests (`tests/`, 7 passing).**
  - The DP matches brute-force enumeration.
  - LSMC collapses to intrinsic value when volatility goes to zero.
  - The out-of-sample value is a lower bound, and contract constraints are always met.
  - The OU and MRJD estimators recover known parameters, and the MRJD beats the OU on AIC for jump data.
  - The analytic forward matches Monte Carlo.

## Limitations

- **Statistical, not risk-neutral, measure.** In practice storage is valued and hedged against the NYMEX futures
  curve, which pins down the intrinsic value. Free futures data were not available here, so the "forward
  curve" is the model expectation. The extrinsic value should be read as a statistical-measure estimate.
- **Spot-based trading.** Real desks also capture extrinsic value through rolling-intrinsic futures hedges.
  This model trades spot only.
- **One-factor dynamics.** A two-factor (short/long-term) model would better capture moves in the whole curve.
  Regime switching would capture the 2021–22 high-price period.

## Extensions

Repeat the backtest over every gas year since 2012 for a distribution of realised performance; calibrate to
futures curves (risk-neutral); add a two-factor model; add a rolling-intrinsic hedging benchmark.

## Run

```bash
pip install -r requirements.txt
python -m pytest -q tests          # 7 tests, ~5 s
python scripts/run_valuation.py    # ~2.5 min: downloads FRED data, writes results/ and figures/
```

`src/gas_storage/model.py` holds the price models, `storage.py` the facility, DP and LSMC, and `data.py`
the FRED loader.
